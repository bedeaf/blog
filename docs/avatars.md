# Quiet Masks

A crowd rendered as one face. Every portrait shares the same pale, smiling mask; what differs is
everything the mask cannot hide: the fall of the hair, the colour of the neck and ears, the cut of
the hood. Identity survives only at the edges.

The ground is absolute black, so each figure reads like a print pulled from a dark plate. The mask
is the only near-white surface; skin and hair carry the colour, kept muted so no portrait shouts
louder than another. Lines are few and placed with painstaking care: two raised brows, two narrow
slits, a curled moustache, a thin point of beard, one patient smile.

Scale is tiny (46 px), so every curve is drawn at 64 units and checked at thumbnail size; nothing
survives that does not still read when small. Variation is systematic, not random: a name hashes to
hair, skin, garment and silhouette, so the same visitor always returns as the same masked stranger.

The author is the reduction of the crowd: no hair, no colour, white mask lines on black. The most
known figure is the least described.
